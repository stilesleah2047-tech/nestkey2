How to Rent a House in Nairobi (2026 Guide)

Renting in Nairobi can move fast, and the best houses go quickly. Whether you're after a bedsitter in Roysambu, a one‑bedroom in South B or a family home in Kilimani, a little preparation saves you time, money and stress. Here's a practical, up‑to‑date walkthrough.

1. Set a realistic budget

A common rule is to keep rent around a third of your monthly income. Remember to budget beyond rent: most landlords ask for a deposit (often one month's rent), and you'll have moving costs, water and electricity deposits, and sometimes a service charge in apartments. Factor in transport too — a cheaper house far from work can cost more once you add daily fare.

2. Choose the right area

Nairobi's neighbourhoods differ a lot in price and character. If you value nightlife and short commutes to Westlands offices, areas like Kilimani or Parklands work well. For quieter, family‑friendly living, look at South B, South C, Langata or the satellite towns like Kitengela and Ruiru. 3. Search smart and shortlist

Use filters for your budget and number of bedrooms, and prioritise listings with plenty of photos, a video walk‑through and a pinned map location they save wasted trips. Shortlist three or four before you start viewing.

4. View before you pay anything

Always view the actual house. Check water pressure, whether there's a reliable water supply, the state of the wiring and sockets, security (gate, lighting, fencing), and mobile network. Ask about the landlord or caretaker's responsiveness and how issues are fixed.

5. Understand the tenancy agreement

Read the agreement carefully. Confirm the rent, the deposit and the conditions for getting it back, the notice period (commonly one to two months), who pays for repairs, and any penalties. Get receipts for every payment, and never pay a deposit for a house you haven't seen.

6. Move in and document the condition

On the day you move in, photograph the condition of the house walls, fittings, taps, meters... so there's no dispute over your deposit later. Note the water and electricity meter readings.
