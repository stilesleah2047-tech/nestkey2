7. Property sales and commission

For properties listed for sale, including land, the seller needs a listing fee or plan to list. NestKey earns a commission of 2.5% (or the rate shown at the time) of the sale price when the property is paid for. NestKey never holds the buyer’s money. Here is how a sale works:

  • A buyer who chooses to pay through the Platform sees the seller’s own payment details and pays the seller directly.
  • The buyer confirms the payment, and the seller confirms or rejects it.
  • Once the seller confirms, we invoice the commission to the seller. It is shown in the seller’s dashboard and paid through Pesapal.

The sale itself is between buyer and seller. NestKey is not a party to it and does not guarantee it. Before paying, do your own checks: an official title search, the seller’s identity, and a qualified advocate for the transfer.

8. Bookings and availability

House hunters can tap Book to ask for a property, or for one unit in a block of identical units.

  • A booking is a request, not a contract. It doesn’t reserve the property, create a tenancy or sale, or commit either side to anything. No money is paid on NestKey for a booking.
  • We pass the booking to the lister, who contacts the house hunter to arrange a viewing and agree terms directly.
  • When a unit is taken, the lister confirms the booking, and the number of available units goes down by one. When none are left, the listing shows as fully let and stops appearing in search.
  • Listers must deal with bookings honestly and keep availability accurate. House hunters must give their real name and phone number, and must not make bookings they don’t intend to follow up.
  • NestKey doesn’t guarantee that a lister will respond, that a unit is still free, or that any agreement will be reached.

9. Title-deed check

The Verify a deed page tells you whether a title deed or parcel number someone gave you matches a live listing on NestKey, and warns you if the same number appears on listings by different people.

  • It is not a land search. It isn’t connected to the Ministry of Lands or Ardhisasa, and it isn’t proof of ownership.
  • A match only means the lister typed that number. No match doesn’t prove fraud.
  • Always do an official search and use a qualified advocate before paying for property.
  • You must not use the check to guess or harvest deed numbers, or to look up property you have no genuine interest in.
